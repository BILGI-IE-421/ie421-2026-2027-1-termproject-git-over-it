## The problem

Refugee populations are not equally distributed around the world. Some countries host large numbers of refugees, while others are the main countries refugees come from. These patterns can change over time, and host countries have different population sizes and economic conditions. At the same time, many people are forced to leave their homes but remain within their own countries. Understanding these differences can help us better understand the global refugee situation and the challenges faced by different countries.

## The data

| Dataset | Link | What is in it |
|---------|------|---------------|
| UNHCR Refugee Data | [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/unitednations/refugee-data) | Contains refugee population data by country of origin, country of asylum, and year. |
| World Bank Population | [Total Population](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/SP.POP.TOTL) | Annual total population data by country, allowing comparisons of refugee populations relative to host-country population sizes. |
| World Bank GDP per Capita | [GDP per capita (current US$)](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.PCAP.CD) | Annual GDP per capita data by country, measured in current US dollars, allowing comparisons of host countries' economic conditions. |
| IDMC Global Internal Displacement Database | [IDMC Displacement Data](https://www.internal-displacement.org/database/displacement-data/) | Country-level internal displacement data, including displacement figures, event types, conflict- and disaster-related causes, weather-related hazards, and annual trends. |

## Our questions

1. RQ1 — Global Distribution

What is the global distribution of refugees across countries of origin and asylum, and how has it changed over time?

2. RQ2 — Host Country Characteristics

How are refugee populations related to the population sizes and economic conditions of host countries?

3. RQ3 — Internal Displacement

How do the scale and drivers of internal displacement vary across major refugee-origin countries?
